# Concept Idea

## Problem
Inventors need materials and manufacturing to build their products, but it is
hard to know where to source them from. They also have little way to see where
a supplier's materials really come from.

## Who has this problem
Inventors and early startups around the world, starting with the inventors I
work with.

## What people do today instead
Sites such as Thomasnet and Alibaba help people find suppliers. In general,
they show who is selling something, not the chain back to the refiner or mine.
Inventors also need small orders, which many industrial suppliers are not set
up for.

## What this project does
An inventor sets their location and what matters most. The site shows who they
could approach, then traces each supplier back through manufacturers toward the
mine as far as the evidence goes.

Inventors usually cannot buy from a mine or large refiner, so the site answers
two separate questions:

1. Who can I actually work with?
2. Where does it trace back to?

## Ethical sourcing
The deeper purpose is to help inventors see whether the parts they use were
ethically sourced, including where each material comes from and whether child or
forced labor is documented there. Most supply chains are not public, so the site
shows evidence and gaps. It does not certify parts or rate companies.

## What success would look like
Inventors use the map to find a real supplier they would contact, and they can
see how much of that supplier's chain is confirmed, likely, or unknown.

## What I am deliberately not building
- A marketplace, payment system, or order-handling service
- A compliance tool or legal determination about any company
- Full coverage of every material and country from the start

## Open questions
- What do inventors search for first, and which countries are they in?
- What evidence is needed before calling a link confirmed?
- Which data sources can legally be published on a public site?
- How will supplier details be kept up to date?
