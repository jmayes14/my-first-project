Methodology

How this map works

This page explains where the information comes from, how sure each claim is, and what the site cannot tell you. Research date: November 2, 2026.

What this project is

Trace the Source helps inventors and early startups find real suppliers and see where parts and materials may come from. Its deeper purpose is ethical sourcing: helping people ask whether the parts they use were made responsibly.

What it cannot do

This site cannot prove that any part was ethically sourced. Most supply chains are not public, materials get mixed during refining, and a company link does not show what is inside one product.

The site shows evidence and gaps. It never rates a company as ethical or unethical. Being listed is not an endorsement.

How supply links are rated

A named source states this link. Shown with an arrow.
Likely
Evidence points to the link, but does not establish this exact step, or the goods could not be seen. Shown with a slowly pulsing line.
Unknown
Not established here. Shown in the panel with no line. A gap does not mean a chain is clean or risky.

How the ethical sourcing check works

We looked for what each company has published, then recorded the strongest evidence found.

Independent audit
An outside body audited a site against a public standard.
Published report
The company reports on its due diligence.
Published policy
The company publishes a policy or supplier code. A policy is a promise, not proof.
Parent company policy
A policy was found only from the parent company.
Listed, not reviewed
A document is listed, but we did not read it.
Nothing found
No statement turned up in a web search. It does not mean sourcing is poor. Small companies often publish nothing.

How country facts work

Click any country on the globe to see what public lists say about it. There is no country score, because a single number would hide what it is based on and reflect on every company there.

Labor
Every good the U.S. Department of Labor lists for that country, as of September 5, 2024: 527 entries across 82 countries and areas. Goods “made with inputs” are finished goods that use a listed material, even if the work happened elsewhere.
Minerals mined
The country’s share of 2025 world mine output for each mineral, from USGS estimates. Up to six are shown, largest share first. USGS names the main producers only, so smaller producers can be missing.
U.S. sanctions programs
Programs that the U.S. Treasury names after a country, or that cover it directly. A program can be a few named people or a wide trade ban, so read it before buying. Programs for worldwide topics, such as terrorism, are not shown.

“No entries found” does not mean a country is safe. The labor list depends on evidence, so a closely studied country can show more entries than one that nobody has examined. These are facts about countries, not findings about any company. All three sources are from the U.S. government, and rules elsewhere may differ. This is not legal advice.

How risk levels work

Risk levels describe materials, not companies. A material gets one point for each warning sign:

  • Labor: the material has a country entry on the U.S. Department of Labor list.
  • Conflict: tin, tantalum, and gold, which are covered by conflict minerals reporting rules. Tungsten is covered too, but is not tracked here yet.
  • Concentration: one country mines 50% or more of the world’s supply.
Higher attention
Two or more warning signs.
Some attention
One warning sign.
No entries found
No warning sign was confirmed. This is not a low risk rating, and missing evidence does not mean a material is safe.

The labor risk view on the globe shades countries by how many of the selected materials have entries there. It covers the materials tracked here, not every good on the list. Risk checks on supplier panels describe typical materials for that type of product, not that company.

These rules are this project’s own simple method. They are not a government rating or legal advice.

How Sayari is used

Sayari is a commercial database of company records and trade data. We matched 21 of the 28 suppliers to company records, checking names, places, and identifiers. No record was found for Core Electronics, MakerHero, JLCPCB, GbE Circuits, SendCutSend, Craftcloud, or OnlineMetals.

For selected suppliers, we looked at who they receive goods from and who owns them. None of the 21 matched records showed a direct sanctions flag. That is a limited check, not legal clearance.

We do not publish Sayari’s risk flags. Many come from a product’s type and country, or from companies several steps away, so they are research leads and not findings about a company. Trade links are marked likely unless another source confirms them. We leave out serious links when we cannot see the goods involved, and we do not publish the names of individual owners.

How dates work

Every source shows two dates. Published is the date on the source, and Found is when this project found it. All current sources were found on November 2, 2026. Some were read in full, and some were seen only as search excerpts.

Older source
Published in 2023 or earlier. It may be out of date, so confirm it before relying on it.
Live listing, record, or database
The page changes over time, so it can differ from what we saw.
No date shown
The page has no publication date. Only the date we found it is known.

The goal is to recheck sources every six months and whenever a company reports a change. Prices and stock change more often, so always confirm them with the company.

How suppliers are ranked

Best traceability
Longest known chain first, counting confirmed links as one and likely links as a half.
Small orders
Single units first, then samples or small runs, then not stated.
Closest to me
Straight-line distance from the center of your country. It is not a delivery estimate.
Ethics information
Strongest public evidence first. It measures disclosure, not behavior.

No supplier pays to be listed. Prices, minimum orders, and shipping appear only when a source states them.

What to ask a supplier

  • Which companies make the cells or main parts you use?
  • Do you know the smelters or refiners behind tin, tantalum, tungsten, gold, and cobalt?
  • Do you have a supplier code of conduct or an audit you can share?
  • What is your minimum order, and do you ship to my country?

Found a mistake?

Companies and readers can report a correction through GitHub issues. Records and caveats are public in the research file.
